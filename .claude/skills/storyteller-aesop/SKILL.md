# storyteller-aesop

Aesop = **the smallest complete story**: two bodies, one prop, one flip, one line. A fable is a tool before it is literature, so every script is **applied**: the moral fits the viewer's day, the prop carries one usable tip.

Optional depth, not needed at runtime: `research/3-aesop.md` (bible: sources, close readings, scored tests, exam). How the set was built: `METHOD.md`. Voice modules: `.claude/skills/storyteller/VOICE-MODULES.md`.

**Brand layer.** Cast voices, logo, visual taste (for example a required transformation moment), which video types may talk, and banned copy (contact details, prices, offer wording, themes) come from your house rules: `skills/storyteller/house-rules.example.md`, or your own file in its place.

**Language.** Write every spoken line in the language or dialect the brief names, then run the matching voice module (see `.claude/skills/storyteller/VOICE-MODULES.md`). The craft rules work in any language. The examples, signature lines and dialect notes in this file are Arabic, written for the example module `storyteller-voice-palestinian`. For another language, keep the pattern (formula, epithet, retort shape, rhyme) and write fresh lines in that language.

## Arabic example: Palestinian dialect notes (when the brief asks for it)
- ALL spoken lines (narrator + characters) in **Palestinian Levantine** dialect. Typical cast: narrator · older man · young woman · older woman. Pick the type to fit a voice in your cast; voice names are casting only, never on screen or in lines.
- House spelling «الي», never «اللي». Easy for Gulf viewers: no rare slang. Use common Levantine: شو، ليش، هيك، كتير، منيح، بدّي، مش، هاد/هاي، شوي، حدا، لحتى، b-prefix verbs (بحرق، بيعرف).
- Avoid Gulf words (وش، الحين، لين، عطني، يبي), Egyptian (دلوقتي، إزاي), Fusha numbers. Check gender/plural on every line.
- The early test scripts in the research bible are **structure proof only**; never copy their wording or dialect.

## When to pick Aesop
- One takeaway that fits in a proverb; a common mistake + its fix.
- Very short slots (15–30 s), hook-first feeds, tip videos, single posts.
- A claim the viewer can watch fail: boast, complaint, "I don't need…", blaming the tool.
- Underdog / inversion stories (slow beats fast, small helps big, seller refuses profit).

## When NOT to pick
- Pride, celebration, heritage glow → Homer. Series / come back for part 2 → Scheherazade. Business wisdom with a sage, layered advice → Ibn al-Muqaffa'. Family comedy, ensemble, a mistake we watch build → Shakespeare.
- **Silent video types.** If your house rules forbid talking in this video type (e.g. a silent fashion or model film), pass: Aesop needs a speaking retort.
- Two takeaways, a product tour, a price list, a named brand story.

## Rules (bible numbering; each one is a check)
- **R1 Two bodies, one collision.** Two principals with opposed traits. A third may only witness (one chorus line max).
- **R2 Type, not name.** Role or species + one fixed trait (عطّار شايب، صبية مستعجلة، أسد). No names. Nature never changes; a fool may learn late, at a cost.
- **R3 Open on type and lack.** Line 1 = who + trait/need + one tag of place or time. Nothing else.
- **R4 The flaw speaks early.** By line 2–4 the weak side states its boast/complaint in direct speech. Best: it names its own flaw (self-conviction: «ما عندي وقت أستنّى»).
- **R5 One prop carries the truth.** A physical object that changes state or owner (coal, cake, cloak, mic icon). It gets the macro shot, and its change of state is the visual transformation moment if your house rules ask for one.
- **R6 One test.** One event. Repetition compressed in one line ("three times… each time").
- **R7 Reversal.** The outcome flips the claim: strong lose, slow win, scorned are right, seller refuses his profit.
- **R8 Speaking last line.** Ends on a character's short concrete retort that names the truth in the prop's own terms, ideally echoing the boast.
- **R9 One detachable moral.** Narrator, ≤8 words, proverb-shaped, balanced. If a moral opens as hook (15 s only), never repeat it at the end. The story must teach without it.
- **R10 Under 100 words.** Adjectives only for traits; no feelings, setting or looks.
- **R11 Flat narrator.** Past tense, 3–9 word lines, no comment, no exclamation in narration. All wit sits in dialogue.
- **R12 No inside, no outside.** No thoughts, backstory, subplot, frame, nested tale.
- **R13 Dry laugh.** Humour = gap between claim and result; viewer gets there a beat before the character. Sharpest form: the fool repeats the boast after the win and gets cut.
- **R14 Built to be applied.** One usable tip shown on screen that works for ANY viewer's daily life. A tip that only fits the story world (a football tactic) fails.

**Anti-rules (any one = rewrite)**
1. A name, a past, or a feelings monologue.
2. A second episode, a frame, a tale inside the tale.
3. A long moral or two morals.
4. Ending on a question, cliffhanger, or narrator warmth.
5. Decoration: scenery, looks, clever narrator asides (that is La Fontaine).

**Fake imitations (judge flags these)**
1. *Moral sticker*: anecdote + "the lesson is…"; no flip, no retort.
2. *Cute mascot*: named lovable animals, happy prize ending.
3. *Motivational parable*: vague moral ("never give up") no prop carries.
4. *Wise-man dialogue*: sage explains, pupil asks (Kalila).
5. *Twist reel*: surprise with no lesson, or "what would you do? comment".
6. *Generic ad* (judge's note on two early tests): a human anecdote where only the moral says "Aesop". Fix: sharper boast, prop speaks or flips visibly, retort echoes the boast's words, an animal/object type when a symbol exists.

## Time maps (VO words ≈ 2.1 per second, minus beats)

**15 s** (~30 words): 0–3 type + boast · 3–9 prop + test (the fix spoken = tip) · 9–13 flip + retort · 13–15 hard cut, card holds the tip. Moral moves to the caption (or a promythium hook at 0–3, never both).

**30 s** (~60 words): 0–3 type + lack · 3–7 boast/complaint on camera · 7–17 one test; the other body speaks the fix (= tip) · 17–21 reversal, prop changes state (transformation moment) · 21–26 retort · 26–30 moral + tip card, hard cut.

**45 s** (~85–100 words): 0–4 hook (type + lack, claim spoken) · 4–30 one test, repetition ×3 in identical framing cut fast, reversal ≈30 s · 30–40 retort, lip-synced on screen (the line people quote) · 40–45 narrator moral 5–8 words + tip card in safe zone, dry beat, no music swell, hard cut.

**60 s** (story ≤100 words + ≤20 for the demo): still ONE test, with room for the tip demo on a real phone screen (slow steps, result visible). 0–4 hook · 4–10 claim · 10–38 test + demo · 38–46 reversal · 46–54 retort · 54–60 moral + card. No second episode.

**Camera (all lengths):** symmetrical two-shot; SAME angle again for the retort (the flip reads as a mirror); prop gets the macro; costume = trait.

## Lendable move (MIX MODE: Aesop as guest)
**The one move to lend: the RETORT-AND-PROVERB CLOSE (R8 + R9).** The host master's own last speaking character delivers one short retort (≤10 words) that names the truth in the host's prop terms and echoes an earlier boast; then, only if the host has no closing proverb of its own, the narrator adds one flat ≤8-word moral and the video hard-cuts.

How to insert without breaking the host:
- Write the host script first; touch only the final 5–8 s.
- The retort goes in a host character's mouth, in the host's register (Homer: still a "winged words" speaker; Shakespeare: the clown or the humbled boaster; Scheherazade: the retort closes the INNER tale, then her frame cliffhanger stays; Ibn al-Muqaffa': the retort closes the inner fable and his «وهذا مثل الي…» stays as the only moral, so skip the Aesop proverb).
- Total morals in the video = 1. Total tip = 1, spoken = card.
- Mark it in the script: `[Aesop move]` on the line.

**Never lend:** the flat narrator (kills Homer's song, Scheherazade's voice), the no-names rule, the four-beat skeleton / <100-word compression (swallows the host), a promythium hook on a host opening, a second moral.

## Writing steps
1. **Brief.** Read the STORY BRIEF. Standalone call: write a mini-brief first (goal · audience · platform · length · shape 9:16 or 16:9, ONE only · product/service · ONE takeaway · feeling · single/series · talking allowed yes/no · house rules). If talking is not allowed, stop and say "pick another master".
2. **Takeaway → tip → prop.** One tip any viewer can do today; a physical prop that SHOWS it and can visibly change state.
3. **Cast.** Two opposed types that fit your voice cast + the boast the prop will disprove.
4. **Beats.** Situation (type + lack) → claim/boast → one test (fix spoken = tip) → reversal → retort → moral. Draft the retort FIRST; it must echo the boast.
5. **Script.** Numbered lines, timed, speaker named by type:
   `1. (0:00–0:03) الراوي: «…»`
   `2. (0:03–0:06) الصبية: «…»`
   Add a card line at the end: `N. (t–t) كرت على الشاشة: «…»`.
6. **Tip card.** The card text = the spoken tip sentence, word for word (copy it from its line). Inside the platform safe zone (e.g. x 86–994, y 230–1536 of 1080x1920); never over a face.
7. **Shot notes.** One line per beat: framing, the prop's change of state, mirror angle for the retort, card placement, brand marks per house rules. Full shot list = storyteller-shotlist.
8. **Self-check** (below), then output: script → shots → card → proof list → word count.

## Self-check proof list (write it out, with line numbers)
- R1–R14: each rule + its line(s); unmet = fix first.
- Anti-rules 1–5, fakes 1–6: "absent" each, or fix.
- ONE takeaway; ONE moral; moral ≤8 words; story ≤100 words (count).
- The prop's change of state is shown (name the shot); it is the transformation moment if your house rules ask for one.
- Tip usable by any viewer; card = spoken tip word for word.
- Talking rule: characters speak on screen (lip-sync); silent video types per house rules never use this master.
- No real person's face, voice or invented words (real events: types, facts on a card).
- House-rules checks passed (themes, banned copy, brand marks); one shape.
- Voice: the brief's language, passed through its voice module (Palestinian example: «الي», no Gulf/Egyptian/Fusha slips); one-sentence walk-away exists.

## Model script: the hostess and the oud (40 s, 9:16)
A sharpened rewrite of an early test: the boast names its own flaw (L4), the fool repeats it after the win (L9), the retort cuts it in the prop's terms (L10).

Mini-brief: teach · home hosts 20–45 · Reels/TikTok · 40 s · 9:16 · takeaway: white-ash coal + small chip · feeling: dry smile, "I do that" · single · talking video.

Cast: الصبية (young woman) · العطّار الشايب (older man) · الراوي (narrator).

1. (0:00–0:03) الراوي: «صبية، أول عزومة في بيتها، والضيوف جايين بعد ساعة.»
2. (0:03–0:06) الصبية: «العود خربان! ريحته محروقة!»
3. (0:06–0:08) الراوي: «وركضت عالعطّار الشايب.»
4. (0:08–0:12) الصبية: «يا عمّي، بدّي عود غيره… الأغلى! ما عندي وقت أستنّى.»
5. (0:12–0:15) العطّار (يشمّ القطعة): «عودك سليم. كيف بخّرتيه؟»
6. (0:15–0:18) الصبية: «حطّيته عالجمر أول ما ولّع.»
7. (0:18–0:25) العطّار: «الجمر الأحمر بحرق العود. استنّي لحتى يلبس الجمر رماد أبيض، وحطّي قطعة صغيرة بس.»
8. (0:25–0:31) الراوي: «استنّت، وحطّت قطعة وحدة. طلع خيط دخان رفيع… وعبق المحل.»
9. (0:31–0:34) الصبية: «طيب لو بعتني الغالي، كان خلصت أسرع!»
10. (0:34–0:37) العطّار: «وكان احترق أسرع.»
11. (0:37–0:40) الراوي: «الي بستعجل الجمر، بحرق العود.»
12. (0:37–0:40) كرت على الشاشة: «استنّي لحتى يلبس الجمر رماد أبيض، وحطّي قطعة صغيرة بس.»

Shots

- 0:06–0:08 · Hard cut: she walks fast down a quiet souq lane to one lit perfume shop.
- 0:08–0:18 · Fixed symmetrical two-shot across the counter: she left, old perfumer right. He lifts her chip, sniffs (L5).
- 0:18–0:25 · Macro, the transformation moment: a red smoking coal; during L7 a skin of white ash grows over it in slow rings, like petals closing.
- 0:25–0:31 · The counter burner: one small chip on the white coal; a single thin ribbon of smoke rises, braids itself into a soft ring, then opens and fills the shop; she breathes in.
- 0:31–0:37 · The 0:08 two-shot, same angle (the mirror): L9 she taps the gold box; L10 he slides it back across the counter, unopened.
- 0:37–0:40 · Hold on the white coal and thin smoke. Narrator L11. Card in the lower safe zone (clear of faces). Brand marks per house rules. Hard cut, no music swell.
