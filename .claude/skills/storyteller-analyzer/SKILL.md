# Storyteller analyzer

## 1. Understand the request (truly)
Read the request and any files it points to. Then:
- Read the house rules file (`skills/storyteller/house-rules.md`, or the example) and note every rule that touches this topic.
- Read `skills/storyteller/log.md`.
- If your project keeps notes on past posts or rejections, search them for the topic.

Answer for yourself:
- What does the requester really want? Look past the words: the outcome (sales? saves? follows? brand trust?).
- Who is watching, and in what language? Shop owners? Brands? Families? Young people?
- What must the viewer DO or KNOW after it?
- What went wrong last time on this topic? Example from our log: an oud post was "boring with no talking" and "an ad for the wedding, not the oud".

## 2. STORY BRIEF (write it, max one page)
```
Request: <the requester's words, quoted>
Real goal: sell | teach | laugh | series (bring them back) | trust — plus the measurable aim (saves, DMs, site visits)
Audience: <who, what they care about, cultural codes that apply>
Language + dialect: <e.g. Palestinian Levantine Arabic · Brazilian Portuguese · plain US English> → voice module: <name, or "none yet">
Platform + length + shape: TikTok/Reels/Story · 15/30/45/60 s · 9:16 or 16:9 (one shape)
Product/service: <what, and what must lead the frame>
ONE takeaway: <one sentence the viewer keeps and can use>
Feeling: <pride / curiosity / warmth / laughter / wonder>
Single or series: <if series, how many parts>
Talking?: <silent by house rule (e.g. model videos) | talking check (with vs without)>
Cast: <who is on screen, from the house-rules voice cast>
Must avoid: <topic-specific house rules + past rejections>
Master scores (1–5): Homer _ · Scheherazade _ · Aesop _ · Ibn al-Muqaffa' _ · Shakespeare _
Master: <lead> — why in one line
Runner-up: <second> — why
Mode: single | mix (<lead> + ONE move from <second>) | two options (<lead> vs <second>)
Questions for the requester (max 2, only if the answer changes the script):
```

## 3. Pick the master
| The request needs… | Lead master |
|---|---|
| pride, celebration, a hero, a "legend" feel, a big launch | Homer |
| a series, curiosity, "follow for part 2", bringing viewers back | Scheherazade |
| one sharp lesson, very short (15–30 s), a tip that sticks | Aesop |
| advice for business owners, wisdom that does not preach, comparing two ways | Ibn al-Muqaffa' |
| family comedy, a mistake we watch coming, pride before a fall, a twist | Shakespeare |

Tie-breaks:
1. The log says which master the requester liked for this kind of request: prefer that one.
2. Don't use the same master as the last 2 posts in the feed or the last 2 log lines (variety rule). Ibn al-Muqaffa' fits many "advice for shop owners" requests on paper; don't default to him. In our first end-to-end tests he was picked 3 of 3 times and failed the human reviewer 2 of 2 (slow hook, stacked tales).
3. Shorter than 20 s: Aesop or Shakespeare only.
4. Tip / demo videos (real steps on a phone): Aesop or Shakespeare lead. They get to the steps fast. Ibn al-Muqaffa' only if the tale fits in ≤ 15 s.
5. Before you pick, score all 5 masters 1–5 for this brief and write the 5 scores in the brief, so the choice is visible.

## 4. Pick the mode
- **single** (default): one master.
- **mix**: the brief needs a strength the lead lacks. Example: an Aesop lesson that should also bring viewers back → Aesop + Scheherazade's cut. Name the exact lendable move. Never mix more than 2 masters.
- **two options**: big posts (a launch, a pinned post, a paid ad, a new format), or when the top 2 masters score within one point of each other. The requester picks.

## 5. Proof
- [ ] Brief has every field; ONE takeaway is a usable sentence
- [ ] Language and voice module named
- [ ] Master + runner-up each have a one-line reason tied to the goal
- [ ] Mode chosen with its reason
- [ ] Past rejections and house rules on this topic listed under "Must avoid"
- [ ] Questions asked only if they change the script
