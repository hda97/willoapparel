# Rendering families

A direction is **an idea expressed through a rendering family**. The source supplies meaning; the family decides how
it is drawn. Pattern-led surface is only one family. Choose from project needs, not from the last reference seen.

## 1. Flat geometric spot

**What:** a few solid shapes, no outline, minimal shading, generous negative space, one accent.
**Fits:** product UI with many states; large libraries produced by many hands.
**Construction:** circles, rounded rectangles, capsules and triangles; fixed radii; 2–4 colours plus neutrals; flat
shadow or none.
**Reduces:** excellently; it is already near icon level.
**Fails when:** it becomes generic faceless corporate art. Add one ownable proportion, cut, curve or colour habit.

## 2. Character / mascot-led

**What:** a recurring character or cast carries emotion and story.
**Fits:** consumer projects needing warmth, habit, encouragement or humour.
**Construction:** model sheet with head/body ratio, primitives, turnaround, poses, expressions and fixed features;
group and deformation rules.
**Reduces:** the head or a signature feature may reduce; full poses do not become icons.
**Fails when:** cuteness appears in serious moments. Define where characters must be restrained or absent.

## 3. Expressive line / editorial hand

**What:** drawn line with personality: wobble, taper, pencil, ink or editorial wit.
**Fits:** projects prioritising humanity and viewpoint over polish.
**Construction:** brush, weight range, line character, fill registration, colour-under-line rules and figure convention.
**Reduces:** poorly below about 64 px; pair with a separate icon system.
**Fails when:** each hand drifts. Lock tools and figure conventions, or commission one illustrator.

## 4. Soft 3D / rendered objects

**What:** modelled objects with soft lighting, rounded forms and tactile material.
**Fits:** premium consumer products, feature explanation, campaign and store art.
**Construction:** fixed camera/lens, light rig, material library, bevel radius, colour/material map, ground and shadow.
The source scene is the system—not only its renders.
**Reduces:** only through parallel flat equivalents designed for small sizes.
**Fails when:** camera, light or material varies, or the work becomes generic 3D stock.

## 5. Pattern-led surface

**What:** patterned linework clipped into clean silhouettes; the source behaviour carries the identity.
**Fits:** projects with a meaningful material, process or notation source, such as security print, textiles or maps.
**Construction:** seamless pattern from source primitives; named pattern owners; controlled density and contrast;
pattern-free reduced state.
**Reduces:** surface detail drops away while the silhouette remains.
**Variants:**

- *Pattern on object:* one source grammar fills selected object faces.
- *Patterned planes:* each figurative plane owns a simple pattern and depth comes from overlap and counter-shapes.

**Fails when:** every asset becomes a patterned object on a square. Vary crop, scale and scene type.

## 6. Technical / diagrammatic

**What:** charts, schematics, blueprints, maps or interface wireframes become illustration language.
**Fits:** expert tools, investing, developer products and process explanation.
**Construction:** grid, hierarchy of line weights, annotation rules, data abstraction, one answer/signal accent.
**Reduces:** well as line icons.
**Fails when:** it becomes cold or resembles real data. Keep it abstract and add only necessary human cues.

## 7. Brand device / recurring object

**What:** one ownable shape, frame or object transforms to tell many stories, often derived from logo or type.
**Fits:** projects with strong existing geometry and motion-heavy product suites.
**Construction:** base geometry, allowed operations such as fold/split/stack/orbit, handoff to type/UI and exclusions.
**Reduces:** the device itself is the reduced state.
**Fails when:** it is forced into every image. Use it as a thread, not wallpaper.

## 8. Collage / mixed media

**What:** cut-out photography, licensed textures and graphic shapes combined.
**Fits:** campaigns, editorial, social and projects with photographic equity.
**Construction:** cutout edges, shadow rule, licensed source library, overlay rules and crop grid.
**Reduces:** poorly; pair with another product family.
**Fails when:** placed in dense UI or built from unclear image rights.

## Combining families by touchpoint

Strong systems may assign families by tier: flat spots for product, technical scenes for onboarding, ambient pattern for
backgrounds, expressive or 3D work for campaigns. A pairing is valid when both families share tokens, one idea and at
least one construction rule.

## Choosing three directions

1. Map touchpoints and tiers with `touchpoints.md`.
2. Score each family against tone, production capacity, minimum size, motion ambition and existing identity.
3. Choose three directions that differ in family or family pairing. At most one may be pattern-led unless requested.
4. Give every direction a project-derived idea. A rendering family alone is not a direction.
