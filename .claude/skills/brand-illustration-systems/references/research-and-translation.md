# Project-specific metaphor research and translation

Use this in Design mode after the project brief and touchpoints are understood. The objective is not to find a trendy
visual style. It is to discover a real-world source whose meaning and behaviour clarify this project's promise.

## Isolation rule

Start closed-book. Do not inspect repository examples, previous projects, worked studies, templates, style galleries,
competitor illustrations, or rendering-family examples while generating metaphors. Those materials create anchors but
provide no evidence about the current project.

A candidate is valid only when it has a written chain:

`current-project fact → shared behaviour or structure → candidate source`

Discard any candidate that cannot complete that chain.

## 1. Build a project evidence inventory

Extract concrete evidence under these headings:

- **Promise:** what becomes better for the audience?
- **Transformation:** before → action → after.
- **Product verbs:** what people and the product repeatedly do.
- **Mechanisms:** how the product actually produces its result.
- **Relationships:** individual/group, guide/learner, input/output, risk/protection, or other real relationships.
- **Time:** instant, cumulative, cyclical, staged, reversible, compounding, decaying, or persistent.
- **Tensions:** the `X but not Y` qualities the identity must hold together.
- **Audience rituals and language:** familiar actions, tools and mental models.
- **Identity evidence:** logo operations, type geometry, layout, interface components, sound and motion.
- **Place and context:** relevant environment, infrastructure, history, regulation or culture.

Use only facts from the current brief, supplied materials, inspected product, or cited research. Label assumptions.

## 2. Generate metaphor territories from relationships

For each strong fact, ask several structural questions without naming a visual style:

- What real process performs the same verb?
- What system has the same relationship between parts?
- What material records change in the same way?
- What mechanism turns the same input into an analogous output?
- What practice holds the same productive tension?
- What phenomenon changes at the same rhythm or scale?
- What notation makes this kind of invisible relationship understandable?

Generate 8–12 candidates from at least four different project facts. For each, record:

```text
Project evidence:
Shared behaviour:
Candidate source:
Why the analogy is useful:
What it could generate besides a surface pattern:
Main risk or cliché:
```

Do not select a rendering family yet. Do not force candidates to be materials, crafts or patterns. A useful source may
produce a spatial rule, character behaviour, transformation, diagram, object logic, narrative structure or motion verb.

## 3. Remove weak and anchored candidates

Reject a candidate when:

- it was recalled from an internal example or another brand rather than derived from evidence;
- it fits the category but not this project's distinct promise;
- its visual appeal is stronger than its semantic relationship;
- it offers only one motif or texture and cannot explain varied subjects;
- it repeats a competitor's familiar metaphor;
- it depends on a cultural form whose provenance or permission cannot be established;
- it fails at the smallest touchpoint or cannot produce a reduced state.

Keep 3–5 candidates whose evidence chains are genuinely different.

## 4. Research each shortlisted source

Research the source itself—not existing brand illustrations using it. Prefer primary, institutional, technical,
community-led or practitioner sources. Keep a research ledger with links, publication dates, and a clear split between
documented facts and inference.

### Meaning and provenance

Search with queries generated from the candidate:

- `[source] history function meaning`
- `[source] origin makers terminology`
- `[source] cultural context archive museum`
- `[source] sacred restricted commercial use`

Record origin, geography, function, makers, dates, associations, ownership and restrictions.

### Construction and behaviour

- `how [source] works process diagram`
- `how [source] is made stages tools`
- `[source] structure repeat unit geometry`
- `[source] cross section close up`
- `[source] movement growth transformation sequence`
- `[source field] technical manual notation taxonomy`

Collect process diagrams, intermediate states, tools, irregularities and failures. These reveal generative rules more
reliably than polished specimens.

### Scale, reproduction and feasibility

- `[source operation] generative algorithm`
- `[source mark] minimum line gap screen print`
- `[source] animation movement principles`
- `[source] practitioner collaboration licensing`

Test whether the source can be simplified without losing its meaning, whether it works across the touchpoint map, and
whether the team can reproduce it.

## 5. Derive a pattern only when the source warrants one

Pattern is one possible output, not the goal of research. First determine whether the source naturally repeats,
registers, tiles, branches, accumulates, oscillates or sequences.

When it does, document:

- the smallest repeat or generative unit;
- how units relate, transform and terminate;
- direction, rhythm, density, scale and irregularity;
- which subject surfaces or semantic roles may own it;
- how the pattern reduces or disappears;
- which aspects are meaningful versus merely decorative.

Build the pattern from these operations. Do not paste a recognisable motif onto objects. If the source's strongest
quality is spatial, narrative, material or behavioural, use that quality instead of forcing a tile.

## 6. Score the researched candidates

Score 1–5 and justify each score with project evidence or research:

- Brand-semantic fit.
- Distinctiveness within the category.
- Number and variety of generative operations.
- Range across functional, sensitive and celebratory subjects.
- Touchpoint and minimum-size range.
- Motion and state-change potential.
- Production feasibility and editability.
- Accessibility.
- Provenance and permission confidence.

Reject beautiful candidates with weak meaning or narrow range.

## 7. Translate the source into a visual grammar

### Strategic input

- Project tenet and customer transformation.
- Source and verified shared behaviour.
- Productive tension and audience effect.

### Source logic

- Function: why the source exists.
- Process: how it is generated.
- Native operations: what it repeatedly does.
- Associations: what it already signifies.

### Visual grammar

- Primitives and relationships.
- Parameters and allowed variation.
- Invariants.
- Prohibited shortcuts.
- Pattern logic, if warranted.

### Application system

- Symbol layer: fastest-readable subject.
- Brand layer: ownable grammar.
- Spatial layer: composition, field and depth.
- Behaviour layer: source-native motion.
- Reduction layer: what survives when detail disappears.

Only after this translation should the source be paired with a rendering family.

## Cultural stewardship

For a culturally specific source:

1. Name the actual community or tradition; avoid umbrella labels.
2. Use primary or community-led sources.
3. Determine whether motifs convey identity, rank, ceremony, religion or restricted knowledge.
4. Involve practitioners when the form is central to a commercial identity.
5. Attribute, license and compensate as appropriate.
6. Document what was transformed and what was deliberately not used.

If provenance cannot be established, choose another source or a generic process that does not claim cultural identity.

## Reference analysis: separate mode

Use this only when the user explicitly supplies an existing illustration system and asks how it works.

1. Inspect every supplied frame or asset.
2. Separate its rendering modes.
3. Measure palette, geometry, density, pattern placement and motion.
4. Hypothesise the source, then verify it from authoritative published material when available.
5. List execution qualities separately from subject matter, metaphor and brand identity.
6. Describe the layer-by-layer reconstruction.

For a single supplied illustration that must be recreated and applied to a new subject, use `reference-transfer.md`.
Reference analysis never feeds unsolicited metaphors into Design mode.
