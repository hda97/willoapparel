# Project-first intake

Send the intake as one message with at most five questions. Skip anything the brief already answers. When files, a
product, or a codebase are available, inspect them before asking the user to repeat what they contain.

## 1. The project question (always establish first)

> **What is the brand or project, who is it for, and what must the illustrations help people understand, feel, or do?**

The answer should establish the offer, audience, context, promise, and customer transformation. If the user gives only
a name, inspect available material or state a concise provisional interpretation for correction.

## 2–5. Ask only what remains open

2. **Desired direction.** Do they have (a) a definite style, rendering family, source, material, craft, or admired
   reference; (b) feelings, fragments, or dislikes; or (c) no direction and want three researched options? Ask what
   quality they admire, not only which reference they like.
3. **Identity and guidelines.** Request the brand guide and useful assets: logo, palette, typography, layout/grid,
   photography, motion, accessibility, existing illustrations, and anything that must be kept or replaced. If none
   exist, offer to generate a small provisional identity system and label it provisional.
4. **Subjects and touchpoints.** Do they have specific illustrations to make? Ask for the subject, action/state,
   message, placement, size, and must-show/must-avoid details. Otherwise offer to propose an aligned set across empty
   states, onboarding, success, errors, marketing, social, campaigns, or print.
5. **Production.** Smallest size, static/motion needs, deliverable formats, deadline if relevant, and who will extend
   the work using Figma, Illustrator, Blender, Rive, Lottie, code, or a commissioned illustrator.

Optional only when material: competitors to distinguish from, accessibility requirements, cultural stewardship,
print constraints, or final decision-maker.

## How to handle the direction answer

- A named family or admired brand constrains execution qualities, after verifying what the reference actually does.
  It does not supply the new project's metaphor, palette, props, or composition.
- A named source such as a textile, natural process, or notation needs research into provenance, meaning, making
  process, permissions, and productive operations. It is translated, not pasted on as texture.
- Partial references become constraints and anti-goals. Vary families across the three directions.
- No preference means research and propose three families with different construction logics.

Never imitate a living illustrator's signature style. A cultural or sacred source requires provenance and permission
questions before it becomes a direction.

## If the subject list is open

Propose subjects that test the real project rather than random decoration:

- one functional moment: setup, search, connection, calculation, loading, or explanation;
- one sensitive moment: error, loss, privacy, risk, delay, or support;
- one celebratory moment: success, progress, arrival, recognition, or community;
- one process with several actors or steps;
- one abstract promise or transformation;
- one hero capable of holding campaign copy.

Choose only those that actually exist in the product or story. Map them to micro, spot, scene, hero, ambient, or motion.

## Provisional assumptions for fast track

```markdown
**Provisional brief — correct anything that is wrong**
- Project: <what it is, audience, promise, and customer transformation>
- Illustration job: <what the images must help people understand, feel, or do>
- Direction: <fixed reference/family, partial constraints, or three open directions>
- Identity: <provided rules, or provisional colour/shape/type/depth rules to generate>
- Subjects: <specific list, or proposed functional/sensitive/celebratory coverage>
- Touchpoints and smallest use: <placements and minimum size>
- Deliverables and production: <SVG/3D/motion/spec, tools and owner>
```

Fast track may replace missing answers with explicit assumptions. It may not skip defining the project.

## Do not ask

- Taste questions in the abstract, such as “modern or classic?” Use concrete directions to answer them.
- For a mood board when focused references or project evidence will do.
- A second round before showing directions unless the answer changes scope, safety, or cultural permission.
