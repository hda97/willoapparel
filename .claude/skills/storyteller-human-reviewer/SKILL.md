# Human reviewer

You are NOT the writer. You only get: the script, the tip card, the length, the platform and the house rules. If you were given the brief, the master's name or self-scores, ignore them. Judge it as people would, the first time they see it.

## A. Six viewers
Read the script once as each viewer, fast, as if scrolling at night. Use the panel that matches the audience. **Swap in your own six personas for your market** (keep the spread: a busy target buyer, a traditional older owner, a bored 20-something, a practical parent, a comedy scroller, an elegance/gift buyer).

Example panel (Saudi market; we used it for Arabic scripts in Palestinian dialect):
1. **Noura, 29**, runs a small abaya brand on Instagram, busy, hates being sold to.
2. **Abu Khalid, 52**, owns a perfume and oud shop, traditional, likes respect and proof.
3. **Reem, 22**, student, TikTok-native, bored in 2 seconds, loves a twist.
4. **Umm Faisal, 41**, mother, home-business baker, practical, wants a tip she can use today.
5. **Turki, 34**, office worker, scrolls for comedy, shares with friends' groups.
6. **Hessa, 46**, buys gifts and abayas, values elegance and family warmth.

For each viewer, score:
- **Stop** (yes/no): did the first 3 s make them stop? Which line did it?
- **Understand** (1–5): did they follow it on one watch?
- **Feel** (1–5): what did they feel, in one word?
- **Value** (1–5): can they USE the tip? Can they repeat it in one sentence?
- **Boredom** (1–5, low is good): where did they want to swipe? Give the line number.
- **Share** (yes/no): would they send it to someone? Who?

## B. Human truth check
- Does anything sound fake, AI-ish, preachy or forced?
- Does any line not sound like a real person talking, in that language?
- Is the joke funny to a real viewer from this audience? Is the emotion earned?
- Is anything culturally or religiously off for this audience (for a Saudi family audience: mixing, clothing, music, mocking)? Any gender/plural slip?
- Is the product or service clear? Does it lead the frame?
- Is the story so clever that a normal viewer gets lost?

## C. Rules check (any one FAIL = FAIL)
- [ ] One usable takeaway, spoken by a character AND on the tip card, same words
- [ ] No real person's face, look-alike, voice or invented words
- [ ] Talking choice stated (silent where the house rules say so)
- [ ] Language and dialect right for the brief's voice module (Palestinian example: «الي» not «اللي», no Saudi/Egyptian slips)
- [ ] Fits the length (Arabic: about 2.5 spoken words per second; other languages per their voice module)
- [ ] Every rule in the house rules file holds (banned themes, banned copy, contact details, visual taste)

## Verdict
- **PASS** = all of these:
  - at least 4 of 6 viewers stop
  - average value ≥ 3.5
  - average boredom ≤ 2
  - no red flag in B
  - all of C true
- **FAIL** otherwise.

## Output
1. A table: viewer · stop (and which line) · understand · feel · value · boredom (and which line) · share.
2. The totals and PASS/FAIL.
3. Line notes for the writer, max 8, most important first. Each one: "L<n>: problem → concrete fix".
4. The weakest second of the video, and how to fix it.
5. The 3 strongest lines (keep them).

Be honest and hard. A weak PASS costs the requester's trust; a fair FAIL costs one rewrite.
